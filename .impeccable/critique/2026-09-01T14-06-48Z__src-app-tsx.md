## Design Specificity Verdict

**The site is authored, not assembled — and the two places it stops being authored are the two places it asks for money and the one place it lists its own documents.**

**LLM assessment.** The evidence for specificity is unusually concrete and appears in both surfaces:
- `styles.css:113-129` carries an OKLCH→hex table lifted out of the running application, with a note recording that a brighter turquoise (`#38b3c0`) was **rejected** because the product does not contain it.

## Overall Impression

The theme flip you asked for was the right call and it cost less than it might have: the token architecture was already built on two grounds, so the light view is twenty lines of overrides rather than a second stylesheet, and **text contrast came out better in light than it was in dark** (landing: 13 failing instances vs 26). But the light view was retrofitted patch by patch — `.eyebrow--on-light` got a hand-picked colour, the logo marks got an `invert(1)`, the compare table got per-column marks — and **the two tokens nobody wrote a patch for are the two that broke, and both are interaction states nobody looks at while designing**: the focus ring, and the hover colour of the primary button.

The single biggest opportunity is not on this list at all. It is that the whole site — 30 pages, an SEO programme, a film, fourteen documents — funnels into a form whose action is `mailto:`.

## What's Working

1. **The token architecture is honest and it paid off.** The light view is twenty lines because every token already had a counterpart (`styles.css:160-230`). The one token that had no `.on-light` counterpart — `--color-focus-ring` — is precisely the one that broke. That validates the model rather than undermining it.

2. **RTL is engineered, not patched, on both surfaces.** Arrows point leftward because leftward is forward in Hebrew and `html:dir(ltr)` flips them; motion lives on logical properties; `Voices.tsx` maps prev/next to chevron direction per `dir`; `styles.css:955-960` records fixing an RTL bug that put the footer wordmark at x = −997. Measured: **0 elements** on the supporting pages compute a direction disagreeing with the document, and on the landing page all 18 that do are deliberate `.ip-num` numerals.

3. **Touch targets and structure are measurably solid.** Landing at 375×812: **0 of 57** interactive elements under 44×44, 0 horizontal overflow, 1 `h1`, no skipped heading levels, 0 console errors. Supporting pages: one `h1` each, no skipped levels on any of 8 measured, 0 horizontal overflow, canonical + `he`/`en`/`x-default` alternates on all of them, and a real keyboard `Tab` resolves a 2px focus ring at 11.4:1 in the reading column.

4. **The printed date and credit line.** `<time datetime="2026-09-01">` plus "נכתב על סמך הניסיון של ניר ברמוחה, מייסד InPlace." On a subject about other people's money, an author the reader can see is the difference between a page and SEO output.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] The focus ring is invisible in the default theme on the three most interactive chapters
- **What**: `--color-focus-ring` resolves to `#003f47` in light (`styles.css:215`). `[data-theme-flip]` correctly flips it back to `#03707c` for the hero and close plates (`:267`) — but `#what`, `#voices` and `#faq` use `.on-light` instead (`WhatChapter.tsx:97`, `Voices.tsx:235`, `FaqChapter.tsx:45`). Their **ground** flips to dark; their **ring** does not.
- **Measured**: `#what` **1.56:1**, `#voices` **1.37:1**, `#faq` **1.37:1**, `#plans` billing switch **1.80:1**, against WCAG 1.4.11's 3:1. Before the flip: 5.68 / 5.08 / 5.08 / 3.61.
- **Why it matters**: a keyboard user in the default theme cannot see focus across 5 tabs, 8 accordion summaries and a carousel. This is a regression created by the flip, on the page's most interactive regions.
- **Fix**: `.on-light` means the same thing `[data-theme-flip]` means. Mirror the pattern: `:root[data-theme='light'] .on-light { --color-focus-ring: #03707c; }` and `:root:not([data-theme='light']) .on-light { --color-focus-ring: #003f47; }`.
- **Files**: `src/styles.css:215, 251, 267, 296, 312, 370`
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable audit`

### [P0] The primary CTA label disappears on hover across the supporting pages
- **What**: `styles.css:1843-1844` sets `.flow--primary .flow__fill` to `--color-focus-ring` and `.flow--primary:hover` to `--color-ink`. In light those are `#003f47` and `#0a171d` → **1.56:1**. Verified live on `/vs-erp/`: `fillBg: rgb(0,63,71)`, `primColor: rgb(10,23,29)`.
- **Why it matters**: this is the sole conversion control on 12 of the 14 documents, in both editions. The comment above the rule ("all the top buttons, on hover, become light") is a dark-ground instruction that survived the flip unaltered. A reader who reaches the end of a 5,000px document and moves the pointer onto the button loses the label.
- **Fix**: `.flow--primary:hover { color: var(--color-ink-on-light); }` — `#fbf6ee` in light and `#0a171d` in dark, giving 10.6:1 and 5.4:1.
- **Files**: `src/styles.css:1843-1844`
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable audit`

### [P0] The only lead-capture form on the site has no delivery path and no feedback
- **What**: `<form action="mailto:support@inplace.digital" method="post" encType="text/plain">` (`ContactChapter.tsx:77`, value at `extra.ts:413`). No JS handler, no `role="alert"`, no `aria-live`.
- **Why it matters**: on any machine without a registered mail handler — the default state of most Windows and Android browsers — pressing שליחה does nothing at all, and success is indistinguishable from total failure. This is the only capture path for the ביזנס tier, the highest-value customer on the site. It also publishes the support address to scrapers on a site running an SEO programme. Carried over unfixed from the previous critique.
- **Fix**: POST to a real endpoint (a Cloudflare Function alongside the existing `.wrangler` setup), keep the native `<form>` and the no-JS path, render three states in an `aria-live="polite"` region, and add a response-time promise to the success state.
- **Files**: `src/components/ContactChapter.tsx:70-165`, `src/content/extra.ts:413`, `src/content/extra.en.ts`
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable harden`

## Minor Observations

- **The English measure is wrong, and the two agents disagree by how much.** Both measured the same 44rem/17px column and both agree English runs wider than Hebrew and exceeds the 45–75 range. They disagree on the numbers: line-box measurement gave 62 CPL Hebrew / 77 English; canvas mean-advance gave 86 / 90. The column was tuned once, for the source language, and the English edition inherited it untested — that conclusion holds under either method. Worth one clean measurement before acting.
- **The site publishes a contrast guarantee it does not meet in the default view.** `/about/` states that text contrast is at least 4.5:1 "measured on the composited image — that is, on what the eye actually sees — in both grounds and both views". Under the page's own fixed background gradient, light small text reaches **3.55:1 desktop / 3.27:1 mobile**; dark reaches 4.44:1. And `.doc-link:hover` is 3.48:1 in light on plain paper.
- **Light and dark fail in completely disjoint places on the supporting pages** — `both: []` on every page — and light fails harder (3.55 vs 4.44) and in more places (3–6 vs 0–2). Light's clean-ground headroom over 4.5 is 0.32; dark's is 0.61.

- **All five font files load on every page in both editions.** A Hebrew page pulls **83,104 B of Latin subsets**; an English page pulls 24,300 B of Hebrew subsets. `RobotoMono-latin.woff2` (32,796 B) renders one string on `/guides/`: "InPlace". **`vite.config.ts:25` hides five documents from the dev server.** The regex `/^\/(en\/)?([a-z][a-z-]*)\/$/` matches one path segment, so `/guides/separation-of-duties/` and its four siblings — ten URLs across both editions — fall through to the SPA fallback and answer **200 with the landing page** (6,678 bytes, wrong title, zero `<h2>`). They are correct in `dist/`, so production is unaffected; but nobody can review, screenshot or QA the five newest pages. This is the exact regression the plugin's own doc-comment says it exists to prevent. Fix: `/^\/(en\/)?([a-z][a-z-]*(?:\/[a-z][a-z-]*)?)\/$/`.

## Questions to Consider

1. `.on-light` exists in exactly three components and means what `[data-theme-flip]` means. Why are they two mechanisms with two different token behaviours — and should `.on-light` simply *become* `[data-theme-flip]` so no future token can be missed the way the focus ring was?
